In 2026, this is no longer a theoretical risk:
- US-UK and US-EU relations are under strain from tariffs, Iran policy divergence, and unpredictable executive action
- The EU AI Act mandates data residency and algorithmic transparency that US hyperscalers cannot credibly guarantee
- Gulf sovereign wealth funds and governments are actively diversifying away from US tech dependency
- UK public sector procurement rules are tightening around data sovereignty
- NATO allies are reviewing AI supply chain exposure

**The window for a credible third option is open now. It will not stay open forever.**

## The Four Pillars (Use Consistently Across Both Documents)

### 1. Sovereign by Architecture
Not a sovereignty claim bolted onto a US-hosted product. The infrastructure (Civo), the compute (NVIDIA B300 via UK data centres), the training (UK-run), and the deployment (on-prem / UK cloud / air-gapped) are structurally sovereign. No US Cloud Act. No CCP oversight. No data leaving the customer's environment unless they choose it.

### 2. Built for the Geopolitical Reality of 2026
The market for sovereign AI is not a niche. It is the regulated enterprise, the government agency, the Gulf sovereign fund, the defence contractor, and the multinational that has just watched its US AI vendor become a diplomatic liability. Locai is the product that exists because that liability is now real.

### 3. Enterprise-Grade, Not Vaporware
First Light Fusion is live. Capgemini is signed. UK Government is signed. The models are on Hugging Face. The app is in production. Project Mercury has a phased delivery plan with real CAPEX figures and a named infrastructure partner. This is a company with customers, not a company with slides.

### 4. White-Label B2B as the Scalable Revenue Engine
The enterprise model is not a services business. It is a product with a services wrapper:
- Post-train a sovereign model on client data (build fee: £200k–£500k depending on model size)
- Deploy into client infrastructure (one-time)
- Usage-based billing recurring thereafter (>90% gross margin)
- White-label the application suite (chat interface, IDE assistant, RAG pipeline, mobile app) under the client's brand
- Repeat. The model gets better. The client gets locked in — but by capability, not by contract.

## What NOT to Say (Known Weaknesses — Manage, Don't Hide)

| Weak Claim | Better Framing |
|---|---|
| "British Foundational AI Company" | "Building the UK's first from-scratch foundation model via Project Mercury — currently the leading UK sovereign post-training capability" |
| "Beats GPT-5 on Arena Hard" | "Strong early benchmark performance on Arena Hard v2; independent validation in progress" |
| "Privacy-first, no data leakage" | "Privacy-by-default architecture; enterprise contracts include full DPA, sub-processor disclosure, and data residency guarantees" |
| "Break-even by end Q2 2026" | Either confirm this has happened or restate as target/progress update |
| Comparing to OpenAI/Google/Anthropic on same table | Frame as different category — not competing for same customers today |

## Voice & Tone for Documents

**Pitch Deck:** Confident, direct, evidence-first. No fluff. Every slide earns its place by answering "so what?" for an investor. The design should say "institutional" not "startup". Dark/premium palette, minimal copy per slide, data visualisations over prose.

**IM Two-Pager:** Structured like a Goldman/Rothschild one-pager. Header with company summary, investment thesis, business model, traction, market, team, ask. Dense but readable. No gradient backgrounds. Professional typography. Suitable for printing and passing across a desk.
