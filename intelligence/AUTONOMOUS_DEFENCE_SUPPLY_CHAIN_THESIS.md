## STRUCTURAL CAVEAT, HIGH CONFIDENCE — READ BEFORE ACTING ON ANY NAME BELOW
The Micron/TSMC/ASML analogy has a scaling limit the thesis doesn't address. Semiconductor
economics work because demand is billions of units globally. Defence platforms (submarines,
UUVs, MCM vessels) ship in dozens to low hundreds of units per programme. A supplier that
becomes genuinely indispensable across every allied UUV programme is still scaling into a
market smaller by orders of magnitude than what made the semiconductor analogue work.
Directionally right on defensibility and margin durability. Oversold on magnitude of returns.

### SECTOR-LEVEL, NOT SINGLE-NAME — THE THREE DEVELOPMENTS THAT MATTER MOST RIGHT NOW

**1. NDAA FY27 autonomous-systems budget — the fund's own unverified July figure is now
CONFIRMED.** The $54.6bn FY27 Defense Autonomous Warfare Group figure (vs $225.9M FY26),
flagged unverified in this file's 7 July drone-battery section, is independently corroborated
by InsideDefense and the Association of Defense Communities. Broader context: DoW budget
requests >$74bn for drone/counter-drone technology, triple FY26; SASC's $1.14T FY27 policy
bill creates a new combatant command for autonomous systems; House FY27 NDAA language treats
drones as core military infrastructure (standards, doctrine, logistics, sustainment), not an
experimental line item. This is the single strongest structural confirmation this thesis has
received since it was opened — directly validates the "Micron of Defence" framing at the
policy-funding level, independent of any single company's execution. Still subject to this
file's own standing structural caveat (platform unit volumes are orders of magnitude below
semiconductor volumes) — a bigger budget number doesn't repeal that scaling limit, it just
confirms the demand side is real and growing fast.

**2. Section 232 drone tariffs — a real near-term date, and a real self-defeating supply
chain problem.** Trump signed a proclamation (13 Aug) imposing tariffs up to 100% on
imported drones/UAS components. Main tariffs take effect **3 September 2026** (11 days from
this entry) — a live calendar date worth tracking explicitly, not the vague "coming months"
language this file has used before. Component tariffs on less sensitive parts are deferred to
9 Feb 2027, an explicit acknowledgment of how embedded foreign sourcing still is. The
uncomfortable fact, confirmed via multiple sources including a dedicated WisdomTree piece:
U.S. drone manufacturers — the tariff's intended beneficiaries — still source ~90% of motor
magnets and ~99% of battery cells from China. The tariff is a real tailwind for domestic
platform assemblers' pricing power (ONDS, KTOS, AVAV, RCAT all initially popped on the
announcement) but a genuine near-term COST risk for the same names until the magnet/battery
supply chain actually reshores, which the tariff's own component-tariff delay to Feb 2027
implicitly admits won't happen by September. This is exactly why AMPX (domestic drone
battery manufacturing, 2.0+ GWh capacity, already verified S88 in this file) and the
domestic rare-earth-magnet names below are the more durable read-through than the platform
names themselves, which is the whole point of this thesis's original framing.
CONFIRMED, same window: China publicly pressed the U.S. to scrap the tariffs, and on
**20 August** ONDS -7%, KTOS -5%, AVAV -7.5%, Unusual Machines (UMAC, unverified name from
this file's own 7 July drone-battery list) -8% in a single "drone trade unwinds" session
(24/7 Wall St, Stocktwits). This is very likely the exact "tariff-driven pullback" the fund's
own 21 Aug session referenced when re-entering KTOS and entering ONDS — now independently
confirmed as a real, named, dateable event rather than an internal characterization only.
Genuine open question for next session: is 3 September (tariffs actually taking effect,
after weeks of an on-again/off-again reversal narrative) a further volatility event to
watch, or already priced through the run-up to it? Not resolved here, flagged for a fresh
check closer to the date.

**3. Golden Dome funding cliff, 1 October 2026 — a real, dated, high-consequence risk to
the whole missile-defence trade, not sector noise.** DoD requested $17.5bn for Golden Dome
