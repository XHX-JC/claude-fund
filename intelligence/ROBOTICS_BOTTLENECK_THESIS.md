## THE FRAMEWORK
Horizon and posture, stated explicitly by James S85: mass production of robots at scale
is a year-plus away. This thesis exists to identify and monitor bottleneck components
NOW so the fund can position early into whichever names actually rerate as scaling
begins, rather than chasing after the move is obvious. This is a watchlist-and-strategy
exercise, explicitly NOT a trade list. No entries are contemplated until scaling evidence
is real (see per-name triggers below) or a name pulls back to a level worth owning.

Thesis borrowed from the memory (DRAM/NAND) trade: the winning position in a hardware
buildout is often not the assembler but the bottleneck component with (1) mandatory
content per unit, (2) rising content as sophistication increases, (3) hard-to-replicate
manufacturing/qualification barriers, (4) a small credible supplier set, (5) real
operating leverage when volume inflects. Applied to robotics: precision motion, actuators,
force/torque sensing, edge inference, and machine perception — not the robot makers
themselves.

CLAUDE'S STRUCTURAL PUSHBACK ON THE FRAMEWORK (logged S85, stands until revisited):
The analogy is honest about its own limits but the report then ranks/weights the basket
as if it already applies operationally. Memory worked because of DEMONSTRATED cyclical
operating leverage on an EXISTING volume base — nothing here has that yet. Four of five
primary names are being re-rated on TAM narrative and trace order announcements (VPG:
$1M/quarter), not on demonstrated leverage. Early is the point of a watchlist thesis,
but track "did the order book actually inflect" as the real signal, not "did the stock
move on a mention of humanoid robotics." Also: OUST and AMBA's current real revenue
growth is industrial/logistics/IoT, not humanoid-specific — a different, more grounded
risk profile than ALNT/VPG/HDS's explicit humanoid-content bets. Don't treat the basket
as one homogeneous trade.
