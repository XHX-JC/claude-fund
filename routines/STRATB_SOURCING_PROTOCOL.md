## ORIGIN

S70-S72 catch-up session (19 June 2026) exposed a structural flaw in how Strategy B
candidates were being sourced. Every name surfaced that session — RKLB, INTC, SPCX/Cursor —
came from the same method: searching for "what already moved." By construction, a stock
that's in a headline for moving 10%+ has already absorbed the catalyst into its price.
Searching reactively can only ever find trades that are already over.

The fix is not a better search query. It is a different temporal target: source catalysts
by their SCHEDULED DATE, before the outcome is known, instead of by their RESULT, after
the outcome is already priced. This file is the standing method for doing that.

S74 ADDITION (20 June 2026): Categories 1-13 below are all CATALYST-DATED — they assume
a known, dated event. They are well suited to Strategy B (short-duration, time-bounded).
Categories 14-19, added this session, are STRUCTURAL — they identify mispricing with no
specific date attached, sourced from forced-seller mechanics, information asymmetry,
alternative data, valuation structure, and supply-chain mapping rather than a calendar
event. These are better suited to Strategy A thesis-building or longer-horizon Strategy B
than the original 13, and are run on a WEEKEND DEEP-DIVE cadence rather than every
session — see SESSION CADENCE below for the distinction.

## THE CORE PRINCIPLE

A catalyst is only a trade if the date is known and the outcome is not. If both the date
and the outcome are known (a headline already happened), the trade is over before it's
found. The entire value of this protocol is shifting Strategy B hunting from lagging
signals (news search) to leading signals (forward calendars, positioning data, and
structural mechanics that fire on a schedule regardless of sentiment).

Every category in Part A below is sourced by WHEN it happens, not by WHETHER something
happened. Every category in Part B (14-19) is sourced by a structural or informational
edge that exists independent of any single dated event.

## PART A — CATALYST TAXONOMY — SOURCED BY TYPE (categories 1-13, original SI-91)

### 1. IPO / SPAC QUIET PERIOD EXPIRATION (validated S70-S72, new category)
Mechanic: SEC quiet period runs 25 calendar days post-IPO. Underwriters cannot publish
research until it expires. Academic research (Bradley/Jordan/Ritter, Journal of Finance):
average +4.1% abnormal return in the days around expiration when coverage initiates,
rising to +6.5% with 3+ analysts initiating same-day. This is real, dated, and mechanical —
it does not depend on sentiment or news flow, only on the calendar.
Sourcing method: track IPO date of every recent listing in or adjacent to the fund's
universe. Quiet period expiration = IPO date + 25 calendar days. Web search "[ticker]
quiet period expire" or "[ticker] IPO date" 25 days out to confirm.
Standing watch: SPCX (IPO June 8 → quiet period ~July 3), FAC (IPO June 8 → quiet
period ~July 3). Both already in the fund's files — neither flagged for this until now.
Position timing: enter 2-3 trading days before expiration, where the academic data shows
abnormal returns concentrate. Exit on the initiation wave itself or within 5 trading days.
Risk: if the underwriters' actual ratings disappoint (rare — ~76% initiate favorably per
the research) or the IPO has performed poorly enough that no bank wants to be first out
with a Buy, the move doesn't happen. Confirm pre-IPO order book strength and post-IPO
price action before sizing.

### 2. INDEX RECONSTITUTION FORWARD CALENDAR (formalized from existing practice)
Mechanic: passive funds tracking an index must hold the index's exact constituents at the
effective date. Forced buying/selling regardless of price or sentiment.
Sourcing method: pull the forward reconstitution calendar directly from the index
provider (Nasdaq, S&P Dow Jones Indices, FTSE Russell) at every quarterly/annual window —
do not wait for a name already on the watchlist to be announced. Russell reconstitution
is annual in late June — check this every June regardless of other activity.
Known dates: NDX quarterly (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, third Friday unless a holiday shifts it —
see P50 in LESSONS_LEARNED.md), S&P quarterly, Russell annual (late June).
Caveat from S70-S72: announcement date and effective date are different things, and the
mechanical fund-flow trade itself typically executes via MOC orders on the LAST trading
day before the effective date — confirm which day that actually is each quarter, since
holidays can shift it (this quarter's NDX rebalance got pushed because the normal
Friday-close execution day was Juneteenth). S74 CONFIRMATION: Russell 2026 reconstitution
becomes final after US market close Friday 26 June, effective from market open Monday 29
June — confirmed directly from FTSE Russell's own published schedule. CRDO (already in the
fund's crash shopping list) is graduating from the Russell 2000 to the Russell 1000 in
this exact reconstitution, specifically because it grew into a $51B market cap — see
DECISION_REGISTER.md for the resulting crash-shopping-list price correction.

### 3. LOCKUP / GREENSHOE EXPIRATION (two-sided signal)
Mechanic: insider/PE/VC lockups and greenshoe options have fixed expiration dates set in
the prospectus. Two distinct trade setups from the same date:
  (a) BEARISH lean — expect supply, avoid long entries into the date (already used: FAC,
      KRMN greenshoe ~June 27).
  (b) BULLISH re-rating — when the date passes WITHOUT the expected selling pressure
      materializing, the absence of supply itself becomes a bullish signal (overhang
      removed). This is the trade to watch FOR, not just avoid.
Sourcing method: pull lockup schedule from the S-1/424B prospectus or secondary offering
filing at the time the position is first researched. Add the date to the forward calendar
immediately — do not wait until it's imminent to look for it.
Position timing: case (a) — exit or avoid entry in the 1-2 weeks before. Case (b) — watch
the first 2-3 sessions after expiration for confirmation of low volume / no breakdown,
then treat as a fresh basing signal.

### 4. STRATEGIC ALTERNATIVES / ACTIVIST 13D (pre-deal signal)
Mechanic: a company announcing it is "exploring strategic alternatives," or a new 13D
(not 13G — 13D implies active intent) from an activist fund, frequently precedes a formal
M&A announcement or break-up by 3-12 months. The formal deal announcement, when it comes,
is the re-rating event — but the company is already flagged as in-play well before that.
Sourcing method: web search "[sector] exploring strategic alternatives [year]" and
"new 13D filing activist stake [month]" as standing rolling queries, not name-specific.
SEC EDGAR full-text search for fresh SC 13D filings is the primary source if available.
Risk: long, uncertain timeline. Size small, treat as a watch-list addition with no hard
exit date until a formal process is confirmed, not a standard time-bounded Strategy B
trade until that point.
S74 LIVE EXAMPLE (category 4/17 crossover): Impactive Capital's rejected board/break-up
proposal at WEX is exactly this pattern — a 13D-style activist push for a structural
re-rating that was rejected, not abandoned. See category 17 below for the sum-of-parts
math run on it this session.

### 5. FDA / REGULATORY DECISION CALENDAR
Mechanic: PDUFA dates, court rulings, antitrust decisions, FCC/FTC rulings all have
public, dated decision windows.
Sourcing method: RTTNews FDA/PDUFA calendar (free, publicly available) for biotech;
court docket calendars for litigation-driven names; agency meeting calendars for
FCC/FTC/antitrust decisions relevant to existing fund themes (e.g. defense, space
spectrum allocation — relevant given ASTS/spectrum exposure already in the universe).
Standing note: this is a NEW sector exposure for the fund (biotech binary catalysts).
Flag any specific name found here for a James decision on whether biotech fits the

### 6. GOVERNMENT CONTRACT AWARD DECISIONS (defense/space specific)
Mechanic: major defense and space contracts have public RFP response deadlines and
expected award announcement windows (sometimes with GAO protest periods that delay
the actual cash-flow event but not the initial pop on award announcement).
Sourcing method: web search "[program name] contract award decision expected [month]"
for programs the fund's existing defense/space names (KRMN, ASTS, RKLB, CODA) are known
bidders on. Track via GovTribe/USASpending/SAM.gov references surfaced in company filings
and earnings calls — companies often state expected award timing themselves.

### 7. SHORT INTEREST + DATED CATALYST OVERLAY (squeeze setups)
Mechanic: high short interest (>20-25% of float) combined with a known upcoming dated
catalyst (earnings, an FDA date, an index date) creates asymmetric squeeze risk if the
catalyst resolves positively — short covering amplifies the move beyond the fundamental
reaction alone.
Sourcing method: cross-reference short interest data (already pulled for FAC, MARA) against
the forward calendars built in categories 1-6 above. The overlay, not either signal alone,
is the trade. A high-short name with no near-term catalyst is not a Strategy B setup —
it's a standing risk to be aware of, nothing more.

### 8. SYMPATHY / SECOND-ORDER PLAYS (post-catalyst, same-day discipline)
Mechanic: when a lead name fires on a catalyst and already moves 8-15% same day (e.g.
INTC on the Apple deal, June 18), the lead name has absorbed the premium. A correlated
peer benefiting from the same theme, not yet re-priced, is the better risk/reward —
not because it's cheaper in absolute terms, but because the crowd hasn't found it yet.
Sourcing method: identify the lead mover's peer set (same theme, supply chain, sector)
the SAME DAY the lead name moves, before peers catch a bid. Use search_contracts /
get_company_themes (IBKR tools) to map peers systematically rather than guessing.
Position timing: same day to next session, before the theme is fully arbitraged across
the peer group. This is the one category sourced reactively by necessity — the discipline
is acting same-day on the peer, not next-week on the name that already moved.

### 9. RE-ENTRY DISCIPLINE FOR NAMES THAT ALREADY MOVED (not avoidance — a rule)
Mechanic: a stock up 10%+ same day is not automatically disqualified — Category 5 of
STRATEGY_FRAMEWORK.md (mega-cap strong trend) already covers genuine continuation. The
fix for chasing is a hard rule, not a blanket "ignore anything up 10%."
Rule: never enter on day one of a single-session spike of 7%+. Wait for the first session
that holds a higher low above the spike day's intraday low, on contracting volume versus
the spike day. That is the entry signal — not the headline, not the percentage gain.
This generalizes the de-SPAC capitulation-volume pattern (T74) to mega-cap news pops.
S74 CLARIFICATION (KRMN re-entry): this rule governs chasing a single-session SPIKE. It
does NOT mean rejecting a re-entry that sits a few percent above a prior entry/target
zone when the underlying thesis is intact and the R/R off current price still clears the
fund's minimum — those are different situations. See the new standing note in
DECISION_REGISTER.md added this session: do not reject entries over minor price deltas
when conviction and R/R still hold.

### 10. UNUSUAL OPTIONS ACTIVITY AHEAD OF A KNOWN DATE
Mechanic: elevated call volume or open interest building on a name ahead of a known
event date, before the move shows up in the underlying's price or in headlines, is
closer to genuine informational edge than any news-based signal.
Sourcing method: IBKR get_option_data / get_option_parameters tools, or Alpha Vantage
REALTIME_PUT_CALL_RATIO / REALTIME_VOLUME_OPEN_INTEREST_RATIO, screened against names
already on the forward calendar from categories 1-7. Not a standalone scan across the
whole market (too broad, and no connected tool currently supports that) — an overlay
applied to names already flagged by date, to confirm or deprioritize them before sizing.

### 11. UPLISTING FROM OTC TO NASDAQ/NYSE (small-cap specific)
Mechanic: companies on OTC markets that uplist to a national exchange become eligible for
institutional ownership (many funds can't hold OTC names by mandate) and often for index
inclusion shortly after. The uplisting date itself is usually pre-announced with an
"intent to uplist" press release weeks ahead.
Sourcing: web search "[sector] intends to uplist Nasdaq [year]" as a rolling query, or
check OTC Markets' own uplisting tracker/news feed.

### 12. REVERSE STOCK SPLITS AS A DELISTING-AVOIDANCE / RE-RATING SIGNAL (small-cap specific)
Mechanic: a small cap doing a reverse split specifically to maintain Nasdaq's $1 minimum
bid price requirement is signaling it's fighting delisting — usually bearish near-term
(forced selling from index funds that can't hold sub-$1 or now-illiquid post-split shares)
but can set up a clean re-rating 4-8 weeks later if the underlying business stabilizes and
the split clears the technical overhang. Two-sided, same logic as category 3.
Sourcing: web search "reverse stock split announcement [month]" + cross-check against
Nasdaq's minimum bid price deficiency notices (public, searchable).

### 13. MICROCAP DRILL RESULTS / ASSAY DATES (small-cap, commodity-adjacent)
Mechanic: junior miners and exploration companies pre-announce expected assay/drill
result dates. The date is known; the result is not — classic asymmetric small-cap setup,
sizable moves on hit-or-miss results.
Sourcing: web search "[company] assay results expected [month]" for any junior
resource name adjacent to existing fund themes (uranium, critical minerals already touched
via UUUU/LEU on the gainers list this session).

Categories 11-13 are higher-risk, higher-variance than 1-10 by design — small float means
bigger swings both directions. Size accordingly if used: smaller than standard Strategy B
sizing, not larger, regardless of the perceived R/R.

### 21. THEMATIC / REGULATORY NARRATIVE SOURCING (fixes the AIRJ gap)
Mechanic: an emerging political or regulatory narrative (a proposed federal bill, a string of
local moratoriums, a court ruling, a regulatory agency policy shift) creates demand for a
specific category of solution-provider well before that demand shows up in any conventional
screener, because the affected companies are often small, thematically adjacent, and not yet
connected to the narrative by mainstream financial media. This is structurally different from
categories 1-19: there is no dated event to anchor on, and no single mispricing mechanism —
the edge is in recognizing the narrative early and mapping which companies sit directly in its
path, the same logic as category 19 (supply-chain mapping) but applied to political/regulatory
pressure instead of a product supply chain.

research this session (macro check, sector review, even an unrelated stock's risk-factor
section), explicitly ask: "what small/microcap companies sit directly in the path of this as
either a beneficiary of the restriction or a solution to the underlying problem driving it?"
and run a dedicated search on that question before moving on — do not let the narrative pass
as background color for the name that surfaced it without asking who else it touches. Web
search is the primary tool; no specialized data source identified yet.

doesn't allow.

### 23. MANUFACTURING CAPACITY / PRODUCTION EXPANSION SIGNAL (NEW S90, James's instruction)
Mechanic: for a defense/hardware name transitioning from prototype to production, a
new production line, factory expansion, automation investment, or a material hiring push
(named headcount, named function) is close to as informative as a contract award itself.
Contracts show demand exists. Capacity announcements show the company believes that
demand is real and durable enough to spend capital and headcount ahead of it, and they
remove the single most common reason a real contract fails to convert to revenue on
schedule, the winner not actually being able to build the thing. James's framing, S90,
KTOS/KRMN comparative review: this is becoming almost as important as the contract
itself as primes shift from prototype to production at scale, and should be one of the
highest-weighted items on the watchlist, not a footnote inside an earnings-call summary.
Sourcing method: same Source Targeting principle as the rest of this file, trade press by
name (Breaking Defense, Defense News, GovConWire, company IR/8-K capex disclosures),
not generic financial search. Watch specifically for: new facility openings named by
location, stated production rate increases (units/year, with both the old and new
figure), automation/tooling capex called out separately from routine maintenance capex,
and hiring pushes tied to a named production role, not generic corporate hiring.
Bar for logging: a specific company, a specific facility or production line, and a real
rate figure or dollar amount, the same discipline as category 22, not a vague "expanding
operations" press release.
Output: log to the relevant name's DECISION_REGISTER.md entry directly if the name is
already tracked (this is a live-position-relevant signal, not just a discovery category),

## PART B — STRUCTURAL RE-RATING TAXONOMY (categories 14-19, SI-92, added S74 weekend
## session, 20 June 2026)

These categories find names with no specific dated catalyst attached — the edge comes
from a mechanical forced-seller dynamic, an information asymmetry signal, an alternative
data leading indicator, a valuation/structural mispricing, or supply-chain mapping.
Run on the WEEKEND DEEP-DIVE cadence (see SESSION CADENCE), not every session — these
take longer to research properly and are not time-sensitive in the way a dated catalyst is.

### 14. FORCED-SELLER MECHANICS (spin-offs, index deletions, Chapter 11 emergence)
Mechanic: the seller is complying with a mandate, not making a value judgment. A spin-off
gets dumped by large-cap/index funds that cannot hold the new small-cap entity regardless
of quality. An index deletion forces every passive fund tracking that index to sell,
regardless of price. Reorganized post-Chapter-11 equity gets dumped by former bondholders
who received shares but have no equity mandate and just want out. In all three cases the
mispricing has no informational content behind it — it is real and not "the market knows
something."
Sourcing method: track announced spin-off effective dates (company IR pages, 8-K filings,
spinoff-specific trackers); track index deletion announcements (the mirror of category 2 —
removals, not additions); track Chapter 11 plan-of-reorganization effective dates and
new-equity distribution dates for any name relevant to fund themes.
Position timing: spin-offs and Chapter 11 reorgs — first 10-20 trading days post-event,
watching for forced-selling exhaustion (volume declining while price stabilizes). Index
deletions — the day of/after removal, once the mechanical MOC flow has cleared.
Tool reality: fully sourceable with existing tools — IBKR search_contracts plus web
search for spin-off/restructuring calendars. No new subscription required.

run on individual names (KLIC, SCHMID). Separately: 13F filings (45-day lag, slow signal,
suited to Strategy A thesis-building not Strategy B timing) from smaller sector-specialist
funds initiating new positions can carry genuine information edge, more than large
well-followed funds. Separately again: credit market signals (bond spreads or CDS
tightening meaningfully on a name before the equity has re-rated) are a documented
lead-lag relationship — credit investors often have covenant/lender-call access equity

### 16. ALTERNATIVE DATA LEADING INDICATORS (patents, job postings, web/app traffic)
Mechanic: a small company filing patents at 3-4x its historical rate in a specific
technical area is a genuine leading indicator — R&D intensity shows up in patent filings
12-24 months before it shows up in revenue. A company suddenly hiring aggressively for a
specific function signals internal conviction ahead of any guidance language. Web traffic
or app-download inflections for consumer/SaaS names show usage changes before they hit a
quarterly print.

### 17. SUM-OF-THE-PARTS / STRUCTURAL VALUATION MISPRICING — FIRST REAL RUN S74

## SOURCE TARGETING — WHY GENERIC NEWS SEARCH FAILS, AND THE FIX (added S70-S72,
same-session follow-up)

Every source surfaced by a generic query like "stock surges [reason]" — Reuters, Bloomberg,
CNBC, Yahoo Finance, StockTitan — is a DIGEST layer. By the time something reaches them,
it has already been seen, verified, and often already traded on by people closer to the
original source. This is true regardless of which paid data API sits behind the search —
Alpha Vantage and EOD are themselves digest layers, just structured ones. Paying for either
does not fix this. The fix is searching for primary and trade-press sources directly, by
name, instead of generic financial-news phrasing.

Proof of concept, same session: searching "Breaking Defense contract award" instead of a
generic stock-news query surfaced GovConWire (a dedicated government-contracting trade
feed) and businesswire's sector-specific defense newsroom — neither of which appeared in
any of the day's earlier general-news searches. Found NUBURU (BURU), a small dual-use
defense name with two operational announcements invisible to mainstream financial search,
and BlackSky's $99M sole-source IDIQ contract from a sector feed, not a market feed. No
diligence was done on either name — the point is the METHOD surfaced earlier-stage,
smaller, less-discovered names using the same free tool, aimed differently.

### STANDING RULE: query trade/primary sources by name BEFORE general news search
For any sector the fund has exposure to or is researching, search the sector's own trade
press directly — by publication name, not by "stock" or "shares" framing — as the FIRST
step, not a follow-up. General financial news search is for confirming and dating
something already found this way, not for discovery.

### MECHANICAL ENFORCEMENT, ADDED S91, SEE P69 IN LESSONS_LEARNED.md
This rule existed in full above and was still skipped on a live, real case (AEHR, S91,
a same-day press release missed because the first and only query was "AEHR stock surge
news," a pure digest-layer query, when a company-name-plus-product-name query found the
release in the very next attempt). A principle that gets skipped under normal working
pressure needs a mechanical trigger, not a restated reminder — same lesson class as P64's
fix for P52 recurring three times.
RULE: for ANY named company or ticker being researched, the FIRST search attempted must
be source-targeted by construction — company name plus a specific product, program, or
segment name (e.g. "Aehr FOX-XP" not "AEHR stock news"), or a direct attempt to fetch the
company's own IR/press-release page. A generic "[ticker] stock news [date]" or "[ticker]
surge/crash [reason]" query does not satisfy this and cannot be the sole basis for
concluding "no catalyst found" or "no news found." If the source-targeted attempt turns up
nothing, general search may follow to confirm the null result, but it goes second, not
first. This applies whether or not the check ever gets to the point of writing a negative
conclusion — the fix is in query order, not in a review step at the end that can itself be
skipped under pressure, which is exactly what happened to the SCAR case at S86 and this
case at S91.
