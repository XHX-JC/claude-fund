## RISKS

**1. Natural gas price risk**
This is the primary and dominant risk. Gas at $3.50/Mcf vs $4.92/Mcf is a 29% earnings
reduction. The stop at $80 manages this but does not eliminate it.

**2. Legacy balance sheet**
Chesapeake's bankruptcy left a restructured balance sheet. Net debt is still meaningful
despite rapid paydown. In a sustained low-gas-price environment, debt service becomes
a constraint.

**3. Chesapeake/EXE rebrand recognition lag**
Some institutional investors have mandates that prevent holding a company with a
bankruptcy in the last 5 years. This institutional exclusion is a real, if temporary,
ceiling on the shareholder base. It resolves with time (bankruptcy was 2020, 5-year
mark passes in 2025 — this constraint is largely past).

**4. Peace deal impact**
A credible Hormuz resolution could see WTI drop 15-20% and LNG spot prices fall.
EXE earnings revisions would go negative quickly. The 20-year Delfin SPA partially
offsets this by locking in international pricing, but near-term gas price realisation
would compress. The $80 stop is sized for this scenario.

**5. Regulatory and permitting**
Western Haynesville appraisal requires permits. Changes to methane regulation or
carbon pricing could increase costs. Not a near-term risk but worth monitoring.
